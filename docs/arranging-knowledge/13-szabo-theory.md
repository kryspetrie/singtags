# Szabo harmonic grammar — Romans, free chords, secondary dominants

Source: Burt Szabo, *Theory of Barbershop Harmony* (1976).  
**Goal:** Name what you hear and predict where BS7s want to go.

---

## Scale degrees as roots (C major)

```
Degree:  I   II  III  IV  V   VI  VII
Root:    C   D   E    F   G   A   B
```

Build chords in thirds. In four parts, **double** one triad tone (prefer root).

---

## Five seventh types you’ll meet

| Name | Structure | Example in C |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Barbershop / dominant 7th (Mm7) | Major triad + minor 7th | **G7** = G B D F |
| Secondary dominant 7ths | Same Mm7 on other roots | D7, A7, E7, C7, … |
| Major 7th | Major triad + major 7th | CM7 |
| Minor 7th | Minor triad + minor 7th | Dm7 |
| Half-dim / dim 7th | ø7 / °7 | Bø7, C°7 |

---

## Free chords vs the P5 law

**Primary tendency:** after you leave I or IV, roots move **P5 down** (or P4 up).

**Free chords:** **I and IV** may leap **anywhere**. After that leap, normal laws resume.

| Move | Legal? | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| C → E7 | Yes | Free leap from I |
| E7 → A7 → D7 → G7 → C | Yes | Circle home |
| D7 → F | Weak / atypical | Breaks P5 highway without springboard story |
| F → C | Yes | Cadential up-fifth (IV→I) |

Minor: springboards are **i and iv** (same degrees 0 and 5).

---

## Secondary dominant map (C major)

A Mm7 whose root is a **P5 above** a target functions as **V7 of that target**.

| Chord | Spelling | Barbershop degree label | Classical function | Typical next |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| G7 | G B D F | V7 | V7 | C |
| D7 | D F♯ A C | **II7** | **V7/V** | G7 |
| A7 | A C♯ E G | VI7 | V7/ii | D7 / Dm |
| E7 | E G♯ B D | III7 | V7/vi | A7 / Am |
| C7 | C E G B♭ | **I7** | **V7/IV** | F / F7 |
| F7 | F A C E♭ | IV7 | *(not a sec. dom. of a diatonic triad)* | C or elsewhere |

### Dual-label policy (app)

- Prefer **I7** when the Mm7 sits on tonic (alt display `V7/IV` when it drives IV).  
- Prefer **V7/V** when teaching function (alt `II7`).  
- Never invent `V7/degN` — use chromatic names (`V7/♭VII`, …).  
- **`m7` is never labeled V7.**

## The classic circle chain

Leap out from tonic, then fall by fifths:

```
C → E7 → A7 → D7 → G7 → C
I   III7  VI7  II7  V7   I
```

**Full circle map, distance-from-home, springboards, multi-key arranging checklist:** [21-circle-of-fifths.md](21-circle-of-fifths.md) (canonical for S11 pedagogy). This chapter owns **Roman / secondary-dominant naming**.

Stevens “distance from home” (as taught via Prietto):

| Distance | Chord in C | Song flavor |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | G7 | Close home |
| 2 | D7 | “Coney Island” territory |
| 3 | A7 | Deeper |
| 4 | E7 | “Five Foot Two” leap |

## A minor sketch

Pillars: **i, iv, V(7)** with raised leading tone for V.

**Pure fifths highway** (after a free leap from i):

```
Am → F♯7 → B7 → E7 → Am
i    (#VI7) II7  V7   i
```

**Do not** treat `F7 → B7` as a descending fifth — that interval is a **tritone** (R3 counterpart pair). A common *color* path that uses it honestly:

```
Am → C7 → F7 ↔ B7 → E7 → Am
i    III7  VI7 R3  II7  V7  i
```

(Choose Bø7 vs B7 from melody.)

## Lowered / raised degree extras

- Secondary BS7s on **lowered** degrees often also move by **dim5 down** (soft score in the app).  
- Parallel BS7s on lowered degrees often slide **chromatically down**.  
- Raised-degree BS7s are often **passing**, sliding **up** by half step.

---

## Dim7 labeling honesty

Classical °7 often resolves root up by half step. Barbershop often uses °7 as a **neighbor** to a BS7/triad. If function is unclear, label **°7** as color — don’t invent a fake Roman root.

---

## BS vs classical (teaching contrast)

| Topic | Classical habit | Barbershop habit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Signature move | V7→I inside the key | Mm7 on **many** chromatic roots |
| Triads | Common pillars | Beyond I, triads can feel “colorless” → prefer 7ths |
| M7 / M6 / add9 | Everyday color | Mostly **melody-demanded** |
| Naming | Strict RN | Same PCs may be ø7 / m6 / rootless Dom9 by **context** |
| Tuning | ET OK | Just vertical lock |
