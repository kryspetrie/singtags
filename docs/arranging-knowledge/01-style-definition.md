# Style definition — what makes it barbershop

Source: *Barbershop Arranging Manual* (1980) pp. 3–25; reinforced by Prietto style blocks.  
**Goal:** Know the hard gates before you write a single chord.

---

## One-sentence definition

Four unaccompanied parts, melody in the **lead**, harmony built to **lock and ring** (Prietto; BAM “expanded” sound), driven by **barbershop sevenths** that resolve mainly by **circle-of-fifths** motion into major triads.

**The sound in one gesture:** a BS7’s **tritone** (e.g. B–F in G7) wants to resolve into a resting triad (→ C). Charts that never create and release that energy rarely “sound barbershop.” Prietto calls this *tri-tone energy*; circle map in [21](21-circle-of-fifths.md).

---

## Style gates (S1–S16)

Treat these as **contest / idiom constraints**. Learning profiles may soften some; `sai11` / `bhs_extended` profiles encode the chord-set ones.

| ID | Gate | Why it matters | Example (C) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **S1** | Four voice parts (brief fewer-than-four OK) | Quartet / chorus-as-quartets | TTBB or SSAA |
| **S2** | Unaccompanied | No piano filling the harmony | Arrangement must stand alone |
| **S3** | Melody in the **lead** (bass/tenor rare exceptions) | Listeners follow the tune in one fixed part | Lead sings the song |
| **S4** | Primarily chord-per-melody-note (homophonic feel) | Parts share rhythm/words most of the time | Block chords under lead |
| **S5** | Consonant chord vocabulary | Only legal natures (see [12](12-eleven-chords-ji.md)) | C, C7, Am, … not jazz 13 stacks |
| **S6** | Tenor generally above lead | Classic “ring” stack | Tenor on G5, lead on E4 |
| **S7** | Bass generally lowest | Foundation / overtones | Bass on C3 under all |
| **S8** | Bari may cross above lead | Inner-voice freedom when melody dips | Bari D4 over lead C4 |
| **S9** | Feature major, minor, **BS7** | Style DNA | C · Am · G7 |
| **S10** | Feature major / BS7 / BS9 as “primary colors” (SAI sense) | Distinct from theory I/IV/V | See naming trap below |
| **S11** | Progressions resolve mainly on **Circle of Fifths** | Forward “highway” feel | E7→A7→D7→G7→C — deep dive [21](21-circle-of-fifths.md) |
| **S12** | Sixth / maj7 / ninth mainly when **melody demands** | Don’t force jazz color | Melody on A → C6 possible |
| **S13** | No sounding **minor second** between parts — **except maj7** chord tones | BAM bans m2 chords; Rylander/contest carve-out for maj7 | CM7 allows B–C; random m2 clusters don’t |
| **S14** | Prefer complete chords | Missing 3rd/7th weakens identity | G7 needs B and F |
| **S15** | Embellish without breaking form | Swipes/tags fit the meter | Don’t add an extra beat |
| **S16** | Just intonation / expanded sound is the aesthetic | Piano ET is an approximation | ♭7 = 7/4 ≈ −31¢ vs ET (derived) |

## Naming trap: two meanings of “primary”

| Sense | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Theory primary chords** | I, IV, V (minor: i, iv, V) | C, F, G |
| **SAI “primary chords”** | Major triad, BS7, BS9 as featured sonorities | C, C7, C9 |

The app keeps these separate: **pillars** = theory roots; **contest vocabulary** = nature allowlists.

## Tritonal energy (the sound people mean)

Prietto’s *tri-tone energy*: a barbershop seventh (Mm7) contains a **tritone** (e.g. in G7: B–F). That interval wants to resolve (standard Mm7 voice-leading sketch):

```
G7 → C
B → C   (3rd rises)
F → E   (♭7 falls)
```

Charts that never create and release that energy rarely “sound barbershop,” even if every chord is legal.

### Worked DNA progression (C major)

```
C    E7   A7   D7   G7   C
I    III7 VI7  II7  V7   I
```

Roots fall by fifth after the free leap from I → III7. This is the classic “Five Foot Two” highway ([21](21-circle-of-fifths.md); Romans in [13](13-szabo-theory.md)).

## Density heuristic

Strong contest charts often spend **more than 35%** of their duration on BS sevenths (BAM; some 60%+). The app’s soft lint (~30% by chord count, plus a duration metric) is a **coach**, not a hard law.

| Sparse (weak) | Healthy |
| --- | --- |
| Long stretches of plain major triads only | Regular BS7 approaches into pillars |
| One static chord per phrase | Circle motion into cadences |

## Part roles (quick)

| Part | Job |
| --- | --- |
| **Lead** | Melody (fixed part); cadential chord role varies |
| **Tenor** | Highest harmony; stays above lead |
| **Bari** | Completes the chord; most flexible; may cross lead |
| **Bass** | Root or 5th whenever possible |

---

## Style self-check

1. Can you remove the piano and still hear a complete harmony?  
2. Does the lead always have the tune?  
3. Are most cadences prepared by a BS7 (or counterpart)?  
4. Do roots mostly walk the circle toward home?  
5. Are illegal m2s absent (except inside maj7)?  

If any answer is “no,” fix style before adding tags and swipes.
