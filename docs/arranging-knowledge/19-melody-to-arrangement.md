# From melody to arrangement — full walkthrough (B♭)

**Audience:** Someone who knows scales, triads, and “V7 goes to I,” but has never finished a barbershop chart.  
**Key:** **B♭ major** (common men’s key) — not C. Chord spellings: [18](18-chord-charts-by-key.md).

**Minimum prior reading (or MVP path):** [01](01-style-definition.md) · [12](12-eleven-chords-ji.md) · [21](21-circle-of-fifths.md) · [03](03-harmonic-rhythm.md) · [05](05-approach-two-workflow.md) · [07](07-voicing-voice-leading.md). Skim [18](18-chord-charts-by-key.md) B♭ section while you work.

This is a **teaching reconstruction** of Approach Two + Three, not a copyrighted song.

---

## 0. What you will produce

By the end you should have:

1. Confirmed **pillars** on strong beats  
2. A **block harmonization** (mostly PCF + circle BS7s)  
3. One **passing/SCF** fix for a leftover melody tone  
4. A **voiced** TTBB cadence  
5. Optional **swipe** into a short tag idea  
6. A **troubleshooting** pass against the checklist in [20](20-troubleshooting.md)

---

## 1. Start with the melody only

Enter / sing this 8-bar lead (scale degrees in B♭: 1=B♭, 2=C, 3=D, 4=E♭, 5=F, 6=G, 7=A):

```
Bar 1:  D  C  B♭ D     (3 2 1 3)
Bar 2:  F  D  C  B♭    (5 3 2 1)
Bar 3:  G  F  E♭ G     (6 5 4 6)
Bar 4:  F  E♭ D  C     (5 4 3 2)
Bar 5:  B♭ C  D  F     (1 2 3 5)
Bar 6:  G  F  D  B♭    (6 5 3 1)
Bar 7:  C  D  E♮ G     (2 3 #4 6)   ← E♮ is chromatic
Bar 8:  F  E♭ D  B♭    (5 4 3 1)
```

**Eligibility sniff test** ([10](10-song-selection-form.md)):

- Range ~B♭3–G4 — fine for many leads.  
- Mostly diatonic; one chromatic in bar 7.  
- 8-bar phrase — good form atom.

## 2. Find pillars with your ear (Approach Two I–II)

Hum the **simplest bass** that still sounds like the song:

```
Bar:  1    2    3    4    5    6    7      8
Bass: B♭   B♭   E♭   B♭   B♭   B♭   C  F   B♭
RN:   I    I    IV   I    I    I    II V   I
```

**Why bar 7 is II–V:** melody C–D–E♮–G wants **C7 → F7** (II7→V7 in B♭), not a stuck B♭.

Confirm in the app; do **not** auto-fill yet.

Pillar chart:

| Span | Pillar root | RN |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bars 1–2 | B♭ | I |
| Bar 3 | E♭ | IV |
| Bars 4–6 | B♭ | I |
| Bar 7 beat 1–2 | C | II (as C7) |
| Bar 7 beat 3–4 | F | V (as F7) |
| Bar 8 | B♭ | I |

---

## 3. Label PMN vs SMN (Approach One)

On each pillar, mark structural vs connective:

| Bar | Melody | Under pillar | PMN or SMN? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | D C B♭ D | B♭ | D=3 PMN; C=2 often SMN; B♭=1 PMN |
| 3 | G F E♭ G | E♭ | G=3, E♭=1 PMN; F=2 SMN |
| 7 | C D E♮ G | C7→F7 | C=1 of C7; E♮=3 of C7; G=9 of F7 or 5 of C |

**Rule of thumb:** if the note is 1/3/5 of the pillar on a strong beat → PMN → **PCF first**.

## 4. Harmonize PMN with PCF (Step III)

Use [18](18-chord-charts-by-key.md) B♭ section.

| Place | Chord | Spelling | Lead tone role |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Bar 1 strong | B♭ | B♭ D F | D = 3 |
| Bar 2 end | B♭ or B♭6 | B♭ D F (G) | B♭ = 1 |
| Bar 3 | E♭ | E♭ G B♭ | G = 3 |
| Bar 4–6 | B♭ / B♭7 color | B♭ D F / +A♭ | as membership allows |
| Bar 8 | B♭ | B♭ D F | B♭ = 1 |

Keep chords **complete**; on plain B♭ double **B♭**, not D.

---

## 5. Handle SMN still on PCF if possible (Step IV)

Bar 1’s **C** under B♭: C is the 9th → try **B♭add9** = B♭ D F C, or treat C as a short passing tone while harmony stays B♭.

Bar 3’s **F** under E♭: F is 2nd/9th → **E♭add9** = E♭ G B♭ F, or keep E♭ and pass.

---

## 6. Leftover / chromatic → SCF or secondary dominant (Steps V–VI)

Bar 7 **E♮** is not in B♭ or F major triads.

**Best story:** E♮ = **3rd of C7** (C E♮ G B♭).

So bar 7:

```
C7 → F7 → (bar 8) B♭
II7  V7           I
```

Spellings:

- **C7** = C E G B♭  
- **F7** = F A C E♭  
- **B♭** = B♭ D F

This is Approach Three **R1** (fifths down): C→F→B♭. Full circle teaching: [21](21-circle-of-fifths.md).

### Optional SCF color earlier

If bar 6 feels static on B♭, insert **G7** (VI7) into C7:

```
… B♭ | G7 C7 F7 | B♭
… I  | VI7 II7 V7 | I
```

G7 = G B D F ([18](18-chord-charts-by-key.md)).

## 7. Full block chart (roman + symbols)

```
| B♭     | B♭        | E♭     | B♭     |
| I      | I         | IV     | I      |
| B♭     | B♭  G7    | C7  F7 | B♭     |
| I      | I   VI7   | II7 V7 | I      |
```

Roots: B♭ · B♭ · E♭ · B♭ · B♭ · B♭–G · C–F · B♭

Check legality ([06](06-approach-three-rules.md)):

- I→IV and IV→I: springboard / cadential OK  
- I→VI7: springboard from I  
- VI7→II7→V7→I: pure R1 highway  

---

## 8. Voice the final cadence (TTBB)

Target: **F7 → B♭** with lead ending on B♭.

Example stack (octave choices vary):

**F7** (lead on C = 5th → bass prefers root F):

```
Tenor  E♭4
Lead   C4
Bari   A3
Bass   F2
```

**B♭** (lead on B♭ = root → bass can take F or B♭; double B♭):

```
Tenor  F4
Lead   B♭3
Bari   D4
Bass   B♭2
```

Check ([07](07-voicing-voice-leading.md)):

- Tenor above lead? Yes.  
- Active tones: A (3 of F7) wants ↑ toward B♭; E♭ (♭7) wants ↓ toward D.  
- No doubled major 3rd on the final B♭.

---

## 9. Optional swipe into a tag seed

On the final B♭, hold lead **B♭** and swipe:

```
B♭7 → E♭ → B♭
```

or the classic prep for a II-tag:

```
B♭ → G7 → C7   then tag starts on C7 / F
```

Keep even eighths; don’t add a beat ([08](08-embellishments.md), [11](11-intros-tags-medleys.md)).

---

## 10. What “done” looks like for v1

You are ready to polish when:

- [ ] Pillars match the ear on strong beats  
- [ ] Every lead pitch is a legal chord tone of its stack  
- [ ] Cadences use real V7 (or counterpart) into I  
- [ ] TTBB stack passes tenor/bass/doubling checks  
- [ ] Form length unchanged by swipe/tag  

Then run [20-troubleshooting.md](20-troubleshooting.md).

1. Keep the **Roman** chart in §7.  
2. Remap roots with [18](18-chord-charts-by-key.md).  
3. Rebuild bar 7 as II7→V7 in the new key (in F: G7→C7→F).
